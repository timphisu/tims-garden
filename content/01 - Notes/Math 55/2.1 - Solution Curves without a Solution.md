## Critical Points

A real number $c$ is a **critical point** of an [[Autonomous Equations|autonomous differential equation]] if it is a zero of $f$, that is, $f(c)=0$. Other names for a critical point include an **equilibrium point** or **stationary point**. 

If we substituted the constant function $y(x) = c$ into the standard form of an autonomous DE, we'd find

$$
\begin{align}
 & \frac{dy}{dx} = f(y) & \text{Std. Form} \\ \\
 & 0 = f(y) & \text{LHS: } \frac{d}{dx} c = 0 \\ \\
 & 0 = f(c)  & \text{Substituting RHS: } y =c \\ \\
 & 0 = 0  & \text{Definition of }c
\end{align}
$$

which gives us an identity showing us that if $c$ is a critical point of an autonomous DE, then $y(x)=c$ is a (constant) solution. Another name for a constant solution $y(x)=c$ is called an **equilibrium solution**.
