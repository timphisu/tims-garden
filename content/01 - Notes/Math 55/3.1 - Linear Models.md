## Growth and Decay

The [[Initial Value Problems|initial-value problem]]

$$
\frac{dx}{dt} = kx, \quad x(t_{0}) = x_{0}
$$
* where $k$ is a constant of proportionality

serves as a model for either growth or decay. 

If $k < 0$, then the solution serves as a model of decay while if $k > 0$, then it serves as a model of growth. We can find $k$ by solving the initial-value problem.

### Examples of Growth and Decay

**Problem:** A culture initially has $P_{0}$ number of bacteria. At $t = 1$ hours the number of bacteria is measured to be $\frac{3}{2}P_{0}$. If the rate of growth is proportional to the number of bacteria $P(t)$ present at time $t$, determine the time necessary for the number of bacteria to triple.

**Approach:** We will be using the growth-and-decay differential equation to solve this problem where the initial condition is $P(0) = P_{0}$ and the use the empirical observation $P(1) = \frac{3}{2 P_{0}}$ to determine the constant of proportionality $k$. 

Note that the differential equation ${dP}/{dt} = kP$ is both [[Separable Equations|separable]] and [[Linear Differential Equations|linear]]. When it is put in the standard form of a linear first-order DE, 

$$
\frac{dP}{dt} - kP = 0
$$

we can see by inspection that the integrating factor is $e^{ -kt }$. Arriving at the identity of the integrating factor,

$$
\begin{align}
 & \mu P = \int 0\, dt \\ \\
 & e^{-kt}P = C \\ \\
 & P = Ce^{kt}
\end{align}
$$

and implementing the initial condition, we find $C = P_{0}$, so

$$
P(t) = P_{0}e^{kt}
$$

At $t = 1$, $P(1) = \frac{3}{2}P_{0}$ which we can substitute in our solution and find the constant of proportionality, 

$$
P_{0}e^{k} = \frac{3}{2}P_{0} \to k = \ln\left( \frac{3}{2} \right)
$$

Finally, the time $t$ at which $P(t) = 3P_{0}$ can be found as follows

$$
\begin{align}
 & P_{0}e^{kt} = 3P_{0} \\ \\
 & e^{ kt } = 3 \\ \\
 & kt = \ln(3) \\ \\
 & t = \frac{1}{k} \ln(3) \\ \\
 & t \approx 2.71\ \text{hours}
\end{align}
$$

### Examples of Half-Life Problems

**Problem:** Archaeologists used pieces of burned wood, or charcoal, found at the site to date prehistoric paintings and drawings on walls and ceilings of a cave in Lascaux. It was found that 82% of the C-14 found in living trees of the same type had decayed. Knowing that the half life of C-14 is 5730 years, determine the appropriate age of a piece of burned wood.

**Approach:** The half-life model is an exponential decay function $A(t) = A_{0}e^{kt}$ where $k < 0$. Using the information about C-14's half-life, that is $A(5730) = \frac{1}{2}A_{0}$, we can find $k$.

$$
\begin{align}
 & A(5730) = \frac{1}{2}A_{0} \\ \\
 & A_{0}e^{ 5730k } = \frac{1}{2}A_{0} \\ \\
 & e^{5730k} = \frac{1}{2} \\ \\
 & 5730k = \ln\left( {1}/{2} \right) \\ \\
 & k = \frac{1}{5730} \ln({1}/{2}) \\ \\
 & k \approx -0.000121
\end{align}
$$

If 82% of the material is decayed, that means there is $0.18A$ of the material present. Now solving for $t$ at when $A(t) = 0.18A_{0}$, 

$$
\begin{align}
 & A(t) = 0.18A_{0} \\ \\
 & A_{0}e^{ kt } = 0.18A_{0} \\ \\
 & e^{ kt } = 0.18 \\ \\
 & kt = \ln(.18) \\ \\
 & t = \frac{1}{k} \ln(.18) \\ \\
 & t \approx 14{,}200\ \text{years}
\end{align}
$$
