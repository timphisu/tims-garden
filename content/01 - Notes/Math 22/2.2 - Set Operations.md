### Table of Important Set Laws

| Name                                                                                                                             | Identity            |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| $$\begin{align} & A \cap U = A \\ & A \cup \varnothing = A \end{align}$$                                                         | Identity Laws       |
| $$\begin{align} A \cup U = U \\ A \cap \varnothing = \varnothing \end{align}$$                                                   | Domination Laws     |
| $$\begin{align} & A \cup A = A \\ &  A \cap A = A \end{align}$$                                                                  | Idempotent Laws     |
| $$\overline{(\bar{A})} = A$$                                                                                                     | Complementation Law |
| $$\begin{align} A \cup B = B \cup A \\ \\ A \cap B = B \cap A\end{align}$$                                                       | Commutative Laws    |
| $$\begin{align}A \cup (B \cup C) = (A \cup B) \cup C \\ \\ A \cap (B \cap C) = (A \cap B) \cap C\end{align}$$                    | Associative Laws    |
| $$\begin{align} A \cup (B \cap C) = (A \cup B) \cap (A \cup C) \\ \\ A \cap (B \cup C) = (A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C)\end{align}$$ | Distributive Laws   |
| $$\begin{align} \overline{A \cap B} = \bar{A} \cup \bar{B} \\ \overline{A \cup B} = \bar{A} \cap \bar{B} \end{align}$$           | De Morgan's Laws    |
| $$\begin{align}A \cup(A \cap B) = A \\ \\ A \cap (A \cup B) = A\end{align}$$                                                     | Absorption Laws     |
| $$\begin{align} A \cup \bar{A} = U \\ \\ A \cap \bar{A} = \varnothing \end{align}$$                                              | Complement Laws     |

#### Example 2.2.1

##### Problem

![[Pasted image 20260119123139.png|300]]

**Approach**

*For part (a)*

$$
\begin{align}
 & A \cup A \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \lor x \in A \}  & \text{Definition of Union} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \}  & \text{Idempotent Law} \quad p \lor p \equiv p \\ \\
 & = A  &  \text{Meaning by Set-Builder Notation}
\end{align}
$$

*For part (b)*

$$
\begin{align}
 & A \cap A \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \land x \in A \}  & \text{Definition of Intersection} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \}  & \text{Idempotent Law} \quad p \land p \equiv p \\ \\
 & = A  & \text{Meaning by Set-Builder Notation}
\end{align}
$$
#### Example 2.2.2

##### Problem

![[Pasted image 20260119130909.png|300]]

##### Approach

*For part (a)*

$$
\begin{align}
 & A - \varnothing \\  \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \land x \not\in \varnothing \} & \text{Definition of Difference} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \land \mathbf{T} \} & \text{Tautology} \quad x \not\in \varnothing \equiv \mathbf{T} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \}  & \text{Identity Law} \quad p \land \mathbf{T} \equiv p \\ \\
 & = A & \text{Meaning by Set-Builder Notation}
\end{align}
$$

*For part (b)*

$$
\begin{align}
 & \varnothing - A \\  \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in \varnothing \land x \not\in A \}  & \text{Definition of Difference} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ \mathbf{F} \land x \not\in A \}  & x \in \varnothing \equiv \mathbf{F} \\ \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ \mathbf{F} \}  &  \text{Domination Law} \quad p \land \mathbf{F} \equiv \mathbf{F} \\ \\
 & = \varnothing & \text{Definition of Empty Set}
\end{align}
$$

#### Example 2.2.3

##### Problem

![[Pasted image 20260119135207.png|300]]

##### Approach

*For part (a)*

$$
\begin{align}
 & A \cap B \\  \\
 & = \{ x \ | \ x \in A \land x \in B \} & \text{Definition of Intersection} \\ \\
 & \to \{ x \ | \ x \in A \}  & \text{Implied by Conjunction} \\ \\
\end{align}
$$

Since all the elements of $A \cap B$ are in $A$ , so $A \cap B \subseteq A$

*For part (b)*

Suppose $x \in A$ 

$$
\begin{align}
 & x \in A \to (x \in A \lor x \in B)  &  \text{Addition Law} \quad p \to p \lor q \\ \\
 & x \in A \to x \in A \cup B  & \text{Definition of Union} \\ \\
 & A \subseteq A \cup B  & \text{Definition of Subset}
\end{align}
$$
