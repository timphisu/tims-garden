# Comparison Tests

## Direct Comparison Test

> [!theorem]
> Suppose that $\sum a_{n}$ and $\sum b_{n}$ are convergent series with positive terms.
> 
> 1. If $\sum b_{n}$ is convergent and $a_{n} \leq b_{n}\ \forall n$, then $\sum a_{n}$ is also convergent.
> 
> 2. If $\sum b_{n}$ is divergent and $a_{n} \geq b_{n}\ \forall n$, then $\sum a_{n}$ is also divergent.

In general, we use $p$-series and geometric series for the Direct Comparison Test.

---

**Problem:** Determine whether the series $\displaystyle \sum \frac{5}{2n^{2} + 4n + 3}$ is convergent or divergent.

**Approach:** Something tells us that this series is convergent so we should compare it to a similar series greater than ${5}/{2n^{2} + 4n + 3}$ that we know is convergent. Picking the dominant power from the denominator $2n^{2}$, we use $b_{n} = \displaystyle \frac{5}{2n^{2}}$, and get the following inequality

$$
\frac{5}{2n^{2} + 4n + 3} < \frac{5}{2n^{2}}
$$

> The original sequence can be referred to as $a_{n}$ and the new sequence we compare $a_{n}$ to can be referred to as $b_{n}$ 

Since we know the series

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{5}{2n^{2}} = \frac{5}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{2}}
$$

is convergent, it must follow from the first part of the Direct Comparison Test that the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{5}{2n^{2} + 4n + 3}$ is also convergent.

---

> [!note]
> The condition for the Direct Comparison Test that $a_{n} \leq b_{n}$ or $a_{n} \geq b_{n}$ does not need to hold for all $n$, rather only for $n \in [N, \infty)$, where $N$ is a fixed integer since a finite number of terms do not affect convergence or divergence. 

**Problem:** Determine whether the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln k}{k}$ is convergent or divergent.
**Approach:** We can compare $a_{n}$ to a sequence that its series is known to converge/diverge. Take $\displaystyle b_{n} = \frac{1}{n}$ and we find the following inequality,

$$
\frac{\ln(k)}{k} > \frac{1}{k} \quad \text{for}\ k \geq 3
$$

since $\ln(k) \geq 1$ when $k \geq 3$ (which is just $e \approx 2.71$ rounded up as an integer). 

But $\sum \frac{1}{k}$ is the harmonic series which is divergent, and so by the Direct Comparison Test, the series $\sum {\ln k}/{k}$ also diverges.

### Estimating Sums from the DCT

If we used the Direct Comparison Test and found convergence, we may be able to estimate the sum $\sum a_{n}$ by comparing remainders. 

Consider the following remainders:

$$
\begin{align}
 & R_{n} = s - s_{n} = a_{n+1} + a_{n+2} + \cdots \\ \\
 & T_{n} = t - t_{n} = b_{n+1} + b_{n+2} + \cdots
\end{align}
$$

where $R_{n}$ is the remainder corresponding to $\sum a_{n}$ and $T_{n}$ corresponds to $\sum b_{n}$.

Since $a_{n} \leq b_{n}\ \forall n$, it should follow that the inequality holds for their series and furthermore their remainders, that is, $R_{n} \leq T_{n}$. If $f(n) = b_{n}$ where $f$ is continuous, positive, and decreasing on $[1, \infty)$, then pulling from the [[11.3 - Integral Test#Estimating the Sum of a Series|Remainder Estimate of a Series]], $T_{n} \leq \int_{n}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx$. 

So in summary,

$$
R_{n} \leq T_{n} \leq \int_{n}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx 
$$

Now based on if $b_{n}$ is a $p$-series or a geometric series, we can calculate the error in two ways:

1. If $b_{n}$ is a $p$-series, then we can estimate the error using $R_{n} \leq \int_{n}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx$.

2. If $b_{n}$ is a geometric series, we can sum the series $T_{n}$ where $b_{n+1}$ is the first term and then compare $R_{n} \leq T_{n}$. 

## Limit Comparison Test

Let's construct an inequality between two sequences and then compare their series.

Assuming $n = 1, 2, 3, ...$

$$
\begin{align}
 & 2^{n} - 1 < 2^{n} \\ \\
 & \frac{1}{2^{n} - 1} > \frac{1}{2^{n}}
\end{align}
$$

Now we know that the series $\sum {1}/{2^{n}}$ is convergent since this is a geometric series with $r = \frac{1}{2} < 1$ but by direct comparison, there is nothing to say about the series $\displaystyle \sum \frac{1}{2^{n} - 1}$ since $a_{n} > b_{n}$. A method like the **Limit Comparison Test** would be more useful in this case.

> [!theorem] Limit Comparison Test
> Suppose that $\sum a_{n}$ and $\sum b_{n}$ are series with positive terms. 
> 
> $$
> \text{If}\ \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}} = c
> $$
> 
> where $c$ is a finite number and $c > 0$, then either both series converge or both series diverge. 

**Proof:** Let $m$ and $M$ be positive real numbers such that $m < c < M$. For large $n$, the ratio $\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}$ is approximately $c$, so 

$$
\begin{align}
 & m < \frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}} < M \\ \\
 & mb_{n} < a_{n} < Mb_{n}
\end{align}
$$

Now if $\sum b_{n}$ is a convergent series, then by [[11.2 - Series#Properties of Convergent Series|properties of convergent series]] $\sum Mb_{n}$ is also convergent. This implies that $\sum a_{n}$ is convergent by the Direct Comparison Test. On the flip side, if $\sum b_{n}$ diverges, then $\sum mb_{n}$ also diverges, implying that by the DCT, the series $\sum a_{n}$ diverges. 

---

**Problem:** Determine whether the series 

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n^{2} + 3n}{\sqrt{ 5 + n^{5} }}
$$

is convergent or divergent.

**Approach:** What sequence $b_{n}$ should we use to compare $a_{n}$ with the Limit Comparison Test? Well, with some forward thinking, our goal is to have the limit $c = \lim_{ n \to \infty } {a_{n}}/{b_{n}}$ converge to a finite value. For this problem, if we take the dominant powers from $a_{n}$'s numerator $2n^{2}$ and denominator $\sqrt{ n^{5} } = n^{5/2}$ and construct a sequence:

$$
b_{n} = \frac{2n^{2}}{n^{5/2}} = \frac{2}{n^{1/2}}
$$

That would result in the $n$'s in the ratio $\displaystyle \frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}} = a_{n} \cdot \frac{1}{b_{n}}$ cancelling out and hopefully converging to a finite value as $n \to \infty$. 

So, 

$$
\begin{align}
c  & = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}} = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{\frac{2n^{2} + 3n}{\sqrt{ 5 + n^{5} }}}{\frac{2}{n^{1/2}}} \\ \\
 & = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{2n^{2} + 3n}{\sqrt{5 + n^{5} }} \cdot \frac{n^{1/2}}{2} \\ \\
 & = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{2n^{5/2} + 3n^{3/2}}{2n^{5/2}\sqrt{ \frac{5}{n^{3}} + 1 }} \\ \\
& = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{2 + \frac{3}{n}}{\sqrt{ \frac{5}{n^{3}} + 1 }} \\ \\
 & = \frac{2 + 0}{\sqrt{ 0 + 1 }} \\ \\
 & = 2
\end{align}
$$

We know the series $\sum {2}/{n^{1/2}}$ diverges since this is a $p$-series where $p = \frac{1}{2} < 1$. Because $c = 2 > 0$ and one of the other series diverges, by the Limit Comparison Test, the series

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n^{2} + 3n}{\sqrt{ 5 + n^{5} }}
$$

also diverges.
