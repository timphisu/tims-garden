Recall from the geometric interpretation of the [[dot product]] that the angle between two nonzero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is defined as

$$
\theta = \cos^{-1}\left( \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\| \mathbf{u} \| \| \mathbf{v} \| } \right) 
$$
and from this we found that $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ if and only if $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$.

> [!definition]
> Two nonzero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ in $R^{n}$ are said to be **orthogonal** (or **perpendicular**) if $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$. It's also true that the zero vector in $R^{n}$ is orthogonal to every vector in $R^{n}$.
