If $y(x)$ is a differentiable function, then $\frac{dy}{dx}$ gives the slope of a tangent line at a point. We can learn about the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y)$ by observing small segments of the tangent lines called **lineal elements**. 

The collection of lineal elements across some region $R$ in the $xy$-plane (corresponding to $I$) is called the **direction field**, or **slope field**.

> A direction field is <u>not</u> the same as a vector field since a vector field gives a direction with varying lengths (magnitudes) while a direction field's lineal elements are all the same length.
